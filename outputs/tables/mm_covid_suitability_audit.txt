4. ENGLISH SUBSET

[LOCAL] English content available for analysis: 0 records, because the dataset JSONs are absent.
[DOC] The paper reports an expected English subset of 4,282 source-content items: 2,168 fake and 2,114 real. It reports aggregate English engagements of 32,811 fake + 26,565 real tweets; 25,888 fake + 18,749 real replies; and 43,048 fake + 41,270 real retweets.

These documented engagement totals are not counts of news items with at least one engagement. Local counts of items with title, full text, linked tweets, replies, or retweets cannot be determined.

5. LABEL DEFINITION

[DOC] Fake items receive item-level veracity labels from Snopes and Poynter/IFCN fact-checking reviews. Poynter aggregates agencies such as PolitiFact and Full Fact. When source pages are unavailable, the claim itself may become the fake-news content.
[DOC] Real items were added from selected official/health sources and filtered using COVID-19-related title keywords. English real sources listed by the paper are @WHO, @trvrb, @MayoClinic, @NIH, CDC, Cleveland Clinic newsroom, ECDC, Healthline, Medical News Today, WebMD, and WHO.

[INFERENCE] Labels are primarily content/item-level, but the real class is substantially source-selected while the fake class is fact-check-selected. This induces a strong source, genre, institutional-authority, writing-style, and discoverability confound.
[INFERENCE] MM-COVID and CoAID are conceptually more similar to each other than either is to FakeHealth: both use fact-checked misinformation and curated trusted/official COVID information. They are not identical, and both risk class-source confounding.

7. MOST IMPORTANT EDGE QUESTION

[LOCAL] tweet_tweet_relation.json is absent. Therefore no propagation edge can currently be reconstructed without first obtaining the missing release.
[DOC] Its name and README description suggest that the full file may encode tweet-ID relations for retweets and recursively collected replies.
[CAUTION] The local documentation does not expose the actual record schema. It is not possible to confirm whether records contain child-parent ID pairs, typed edges, nested trees, only membership lists, or sufficient directionality to build node=tweet and edge=reply-to/retweet-of graphs.

Even if direct pairs are present in the full release, the result would be an observed partial response/resharing graph, not automatically a complete diffusion cascade. The paper says replies were approximated by searching tweet IDs and could only recover replies from the last week, creating explicit incompleteness.

11. MM-COVID VS FAKEHEALTH PRIMARY VARIABLES

  observed post count: documented as potentially derivable in MM-COVID; directly derivable in FakeHealth; unavailable locally in MM-COVID
  unique post count: potentially derivable if IDs are present; unavailable locally
  reply count: potentially derivable; unavailable locally; FakeHealth has reply-ID membership counts
  retweet count: potentially derivable and would align better with FakeHealth than CoAID; unavailable locally
  engagement presence: potentially derivable; unavailable locally
  first/last engagement time: would require IDs or explicit times; unavailable locally
  engagement lifespan: would require validated times; unavailable locally
  publication-to-first-engagement delay: would require both publication dates and validated tweet times; unavailable locally

[INFERENCE] The full MM-COVID release might offer better structural alignment with FakeHealth for tweets, replies, and retweets. Whether definitions and edge directions are consistent cannot be decided until the JSON schemas and linkage coverage are inspected.

12. RECOMMENDATION

Recommendation C: Keep CoAID and do not use MM-COVID in its current local form.

What MM-COVID genuinely promises: a larger English COVID subset, multilingual context, retweets, recursive replies, richer social context, and potentially more direct response/resharing structure than CoAID.

What it makes worse: strong fake-vs-real source-selection differences; possible claim/article/social-post genre mixing; reply collection explicitly limited by a last-week search approximation; and presently zero empirically usable records.

Richer propagation locally: no. The critical relation files are absent, so the advertised edge structure cannot be verified or reconstructed.

Hydration: the documented full release exposes tweet IDs rather than tweet objects. Edge counts might not require hydration if direct relations are actually encoded, but user identities/metadata, explicit tweet fields, and possibly timestamps would still require hydration. This cannot be confirmed from the current download.

Least-confounded actionable choice: retain the already inspected and processed CoAID as the primary COVID dataset. Do not add MM-COVID as a robustness dataset until the three official JSON files are downloaded and re-audited. If they are later supplied, revisit option B before considering replacement.
